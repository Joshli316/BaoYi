# Common University Student Health Insurance Plan (SHIP) Structures

> Research compiled April 2026. Data drawn from publicly available plan summaries at major U.S. universities for the 2025-2026 academic year.

---

## 1. What Is a SHIP?

A Student Health Insurance Plan (SHIP) is a health insurance policy offered -- and often mandated -- by a college or university for its enrolled students. Most schools auto-enroll students into SHIP and add the premium to the tuition bill. Students who can demonstrate equivalent coverage through a parent's plan, employer plan, or ACA Marketplace plan may submit a **waiver** by a deadline (typically the third week of classes). International students on F-1 or J-1 visas are almost universally **required** to carry SHIP and usually cannot waive it.

SHIP plans are ACA-compliant: they cover essential health benefits, cannot impose annual or lifetime caps, and must cover pre-existing conditions without waiting periods ([HealthInsurance.org](https://www.healthinsurance.org/obamacare/student-health-insurance-required-reading/)).

**Private insurance alternatives:** Third-party international student insurance plans (e.g., from American Visitor Insurance) can cost $30-$124/month ($360-$1,488/year), but many universities will not accept them as waiver-eligible because they lack full ACA compliance ([American Visitor Insurance](https://www.americanvisitorinsurance.com/international-student-insurance/students-medical-insurance-cost.asp)).

## 3. Typical Coverage Details

The following reference numbers come from the University of Iowa's SHIP (Alliance Select PPO), which is representative of mid-range public university plans ([University of Iowa HR](https://hr.uiowa.edu/benefits/health-insurance/student-health-insurance/student-health-insurance-plan-ship/ship-copays)).

### Cost-Sharing Summary

| Feature | In-Network | Out-of-Network |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual deductible** (individual) | $500 | $500+ (often higher) |
| **Annual deductible** (family) | $1,000 | $1,000+ |
| **Coinsurance** (plan pays / you pay) | 80/20 | 60/40 |
| **Out-of-pocket maximum** (individual) | $5,000 | Higher or unlimited |
| **Out-of-pocket maximum** (family) | $10,000 | Higher or unlimited |
| **Primary care copay** | $25 | Deductible + 40% |
| **Specialist copay** | $50 | Deductible + 40% |
| **Urgent care copay** | $25 | Deductible + 40% |
| **Emergency room copay** | $150 (waived if admitted) | $150 (same) |
| **Preventive care** | $0 (100% covered) | Not covered at 100% |

### Ranges Across Universities

| Feature | Low End | Typical | High End |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual deductible | $0 (UC system) | $150-$500 | $1,000+ |
| OOP maximum (individual) | $3,000 | $5,000-$6,350 | $8,000+ |
| Primary care copay | $0 (on-campus clinic) | $15-$30 | $50 |
| ER copay | $75 | $150-$250 | $500 |

Stanford's Cardinal Care plan stands out with a very low $150 individual deductible. MIT's deductible is moving from $150 to $175 for 2026 ([MIT HR](https://hr.mit.edu/benefits/health/plans)). UC system plans historically had no deductible for the base tier.

## 4. How SHIP Networks Work

### PPO vs. HMO

Most university SHIP plans use a **PPO (Preferred Provider Organization)** structure, which allows students to see any provider without a referral but incentivizes in-network care through lower cost-sharing. This is the dominant model because:

- Students travel for breaks, internships, and study abroad -- PPOs work nationwide
- Students may need specialists without waiting for referrals
- Campus health centers serve as the de facto "primary care" hub regardless

A smaller number of schools use **HMO** or **EPO** models, which restrict care to in-network providers except in emergencies. HMOs have lower premiums but require referrals for specialists ([UnitedHealthcare](https://www.uhc.com/understanding-health-insurance/types-of-health-insurance/understanding-hmo-ppo-epo-pos)).

### Tiered Networks

Some plans use a tiered approach:

- **Tier 1:** On-campus student health center -- lowest or zero cost-sharing
- **Tier 2:** Preferred community providers -- standard copays/coinsurance
- **Tier 3:** Out-of-network -- highest cost-sharing or balance billing

USC's Aetna SHIP, for example, covers Keck Medicine of USC (Tier 1) at 90% of costs ([USC SHIP](https://sites.usc.edu/ship/)).

### In-Network vs. Out-of-Network Impact

| Scenario | In-Network | Out-of-Network |
|---|---|---|
| Coinsurance | 80/20 typical | 60/40 typical |
| Deductible | $150-$500 | Often 2x in-network |
| Balance billing | Not allowed | Provider can bill difference |
| OOP max | Counts toward cap | May not count, or separate cap |

Texas A&M's plan covers both in-network and out-of-network care, but at significantly different rates ([Texas A&M UHS](https://uhs.tamu.edu/medical/insurance.html)). UMass Lowell uses a Blue Cross Blue Shield PPO ([UMass Lowell](https://www.uml.edu/student-services/health/health-insurance/)).

## 5. Common Exclusions and Limitations

Most SHIP plans **do not cover** the following:

- **Dental care** (separate dental plan usually available)
- **Vision care** (eye exams and glasses; some plans include basic vision)
- **Cosmetic surgery** and elective procedures
- **Infertility treatment** (IVF, egg freezing)
- **Weight loss surgery** and programs
- **Hearing aids** (some plans cover partially)
- **Extended home nursing** / custodial care
- **Experimental or investigational treatments**
- **Over-the-counter medications** purchased at retail
- **Services at out-of-network pharmacies** (UC SHIP specifically excludes these)

Source: [University of Iowa SHIP](https://hr.uiowa.edu/benefits/health-insurance/student-health-insurance/student-health-insurance-plan-ship/ship-copays)

### Limitations

- **Referral requirements:** Some plans (especially UC SHIP) require a written referral from Student Health Services before seeing specialists or getting non-emergency mental health treatment ([UC SHIP](https://www.ucop.edu/uc-health/departments/uc-student-health-insurance-plan-uc-ship.html))
- **Prior authorization:** Hospitalizations, advanced imaging, and specialty drugs often require pre-approval
- **Annual visit limits:** Some services (chiropractic, physical therapy) may be capped at 20-30 visits/year

## 6. Prescription Drug Coverage

SHIP plans typically use a **tiered formulary** system. The University of Iowa's plan is representative:

| Tier | Drug Type | Coinsurance |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Generic | 25% |
| Tier 2 | Preferred brand | 30% |
| Tier 3 | Non-preferred brand | 50% |

### Key Details

- **On-campus pharmacy discounts:** Colorado State SHIP charges only $5 per non-specialty medication at the campus pharmacy (after the first $500 in refills) ([CSU Health Network](https://health.colostate.edu/domestic-student-health-insurance-plan-ship/))
- **Specialty medications:** Often not available at campus pharmacies; must use designated specialty pharmacies with prior authorization
- **Mail-order:** Many plans offer 90-day supplies via mail at reduced cost
- **Formulary restrictions:** Not all drugs are covered; plans maintain a formulary (approved drug list) and may require step therapy (trying cheaper alternatives first)
- **OTC medications:** Not covered when purchased at retail stores

Prescription costs count toward the combined medical/pharmacy out-of-pocket maximum in most plans.

## 7. Mental Health and Behavioral Health Coverage

Post-ACA, SHIP plans must provide **mental health parity** -- covering mental health and substance use disorders at the same level as medical/surgical benefits.

### Typical Coverage

| Service | Typical Cost-Sharing |
|---|---|
| Outpatient therapy (in-network) | $0-$25 copay |
| Psychiatry visit | $25-$50 copay |
| On-campus counseling | $0 (often included in health fee) |
| Telehealth behavioral health | $0 (e.g., AcademicLiveCare) |
| Inpatient mental health | Same as medical inpatient |
| Substance abuse treatment | Same as medical |

### Notable Examples

- **Colorado State SHIP:** Mental health care (psychiatry, behavioral health, counseling) has **$0 copay, $0 coinsurance, $0 deductible** for in-person outpatient care via Anthem in-network providers. Students can also use AcademicLiveCare for $0/visit ([CSU Health Network](https://health.colostate.edu/international-student-health-insurance-plan/))
- **University of Iowa SHIP:** Mental health copay is **$0** in-network
- **UC SHIP:** Covers mental/nervous disorders and substance abuse, but requires a written referral from Student Health or Counseling Services before non-emergency treatment ([UC SHIP UCSD](https://shwadmin.ucsd.edu/uc-ship/use/medical-plan.html))

### Access Considerations

- Many campuses offer **short-term counseling** (6-12 sessions) free through the campus counseling center, separate from SHIP
- **Telehealth** mental health is increasingly included at $0 cost
- Wait times for campus counseling can be long; SHIP coverage for off-campus therapists is important

## 8. Pre-Existing Condition Coverage

**All ACA-compliant SHIP plans cover pre-existing conditions with no waiting period.** This is a significant protection for students, especially international students who may arrive with ongoing medical needs.

Key points:
- No exclusions for conditions diagnosed before enrollment
- No waiting periods before coverage begins
- No higher premiums based on health status
- Applies to both physical and mental health conditions

**Important caveat for non-SHIP alternatives:** Some low-cost international student insurance plans that are NOT ACA-compliant may exclude pre-existing conditions or impose 6-12 month waiting periods. This is why many universities refuse to accept these plans as waiver-eligible ([American Visitor Insurance](https://www.americanvisitorinsurance.com/blog/student-pre-existing-conditions-coverage.asp), [InternationalStudentInsurance.com](https://www.internationalstudentinsurance.com/best-practices/insurance-options.php)).

## 9. Major SHIP Underwriters

Three insurers dominate the university SHIP market:

| Insurer | Notable University Clients | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Aetna Student Health** | USC, The New School, many private universities | National PPO network; integrated mental health and reproductive health; dedicated student portal ([Aetna Student Health](https://www.aetnastudenthealth.com/)) |
| **UnitedHealthcare StudentResources (UHCSR)** | Penn State, ASU, many large public universities | Nationwide coverage; top-tier medical policy; online provider directory; claims portal ([UHCSR](https://www.uhcsr.com/)) |
| **Blue Cross Blue Shield (Student Blue)** | ECU, UMass Lowell, UNC System, many state universities | Extensive regional and national networks; "Student Blue" brand for university market ([ECU Student Health](https://studenthealth.ecu.edu/ship/)) |

### Other Players

- **Anthem** (Blue Cross affiliate): Colorado State, several Midwest universities
- **Wellfleet (formerly Chickering Group)**: Smaller private colleges
- **Academic Health Plans (AHP)**: Stanford (Cardinal Care), MIT -- serves as third-party administrator, often with Aetna or Blue Cross underwriting ([Stanford](https://stanford.myahpcare.com/waiver), [MIT](https://mit.myahpcare.com/))
- **Gallagher Student Health**: Hofstra, smaller schools -- acts as broker/administrator ([Gallagher](https://go.gallagherstudent.com/))

Universities typically issue multi-year contracts with these insurers. The insurer provides the network, claims processing, and customer service; the university negotiates plan design and premium levels.

## 10. Coverage During Breaks, Summer, and Study Abroad

### Academic Breaks

- **Fall/winter/spring breaks:** Coverage continues uninterrupted for enrolled students
- **Summer:** Most SHIP plans bundle summer coverage with the spring semester billing. At UC campuses, students enrolled in spring have coverage through the start of fall quarter ([UC SHIP UCSD](https://shwadmin.ucsd.edu/uc-ship/index.html)). Penn State bills spring+summer together ($2,221)
- **Graduation gap:** Coverage typically extends through August 31 for spring graduates, giving time to transition to employer or Marketplace plans

### Study Abroad

- **PPO-based plans:** Generally provide out-of-network coverage worldwide, but at higher cost-sharing rates
- **Emergency coverage:** Most SHIP plans cover emergency care anywhere in the world
- **Supplemental travel insurance:** Many universities recommend or require separate travel medical insurance for study abroad, such as VISIT EXPLORER, which includes mental health coverage ([CDC Travel Health](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/yellowbook/2020/travel-for-work-other-reasons/study-abroad-and-other-international-student-travel))
- **Medical evacuation:** Not always included in SHIP; often covered by supplemental travel policies

### Leave of Absence

- Students who take a leave of absence generally lose SHIP eligibility
- Some plans allow purchase of up to 3 months of continuation coverage
- COBRA-like continuation may be available but is expensive

## 11. SHIP vs. Other Insurance Options

| Feature | University SHIP | Parent's Plan (to age 26) | ACA Marketplace | Private Intl Student Plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ACA compliant** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Often no |
| **Pre-existing conditions** | Covered | Covered | Covered | May exclude |
| **Campus clinic integration** | Full | Partial | Partial | Minimal |
| **Nationwide network** | Yes (PPO) | Varies | Varies by plan | Limited |
| **Mental health parity** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Often limited |
| **Cost** | $2,000-$8,500/yr | $0 if on parent's | $0-$4,000/yr (with subsidies) | $360-$1,500/yr |
| **Study abroad coverage** | Limited | Usually none | None | Varies |

---

## Key Takeaways for Students

1. **SHIP is usually good-quality insurance** -- ACA-compliant, broad networks, integrated with campus health services
2. **International students typically cannot opt out** -- and should not want to, given pre-existing condition protections
3. **On-campus care is cheapest** -- $0 copays at the student health center under most plans
4. **Mental health is well-covered** -- $0 copay at many schools, but referral requirements vary
5. **Read the exclusions** -- dental, vision, and fertility treatment are almost never included
6. **Summer coverage is usually included** -- but verify your specific plan's terms
7. **Study abroad needs supplemental coverage** -- SHIP alone is usually insufficient for international travel

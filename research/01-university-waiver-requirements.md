# University SHIP Waiver Requirements for International Students

## Overview

Nearly every U.S. university requires students to carry health insurance. Most schools operate a **Student Health Insurance Plan (SHIP)** --- sometimes branded differently (UC SHIP, Cardinal Care at Stanford, Aetna Student Health at USC/Columbia) --- and automatically enroll all students, charging the premium to their student account. Students who already have qualifying coverage may apply to **waive** SHIP and avoid the charge, but the alternative plan must pass a strict set of criteria. For international students on F-1 or J-1 visas, these criteria are significantly harder to meet than for domestic students.

This report synthesizes waiver requirements from nine major universities (UCLA, UC Berkeley, UCSD, USC, NYU, Columbia, Stanford, MIT, and University of Michigan) based on their official policies as of the 2025--2026 academic year.

---

## 1. Typical Waiver Criteria

Universities share a common core of requirements, though exact thresholds vary. The table below summarizes the most common criteria across institutions.

### 1.1 Universal Requirements (All Students)

| Criteria | Typical Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **In-network access** | Primary care + hospital within 40--50 miles of campus | UCSD uses 40 mi; UC Berkeley and USC use 50 mi |
| **Out-of-pocket maximum** | $8,700--$10,600 individual; $17,400--$21,200 family | UC system: $10,600/$21,200; UCSD: $8,700/$17,400 |
| **Annual/lifetime benefit limits** | No per-condition or lifetime dollar maximums | Mirrors ACA "essential health benefits" rules |
| **Pre-existing conditions** | No exclusions or limitations; any waiting period must have already expired | Standard across all surveyed schools |
| **Mental health & substance abuse** | Full parity with medical/surgical benefits; must cover inpatient + outpatient | Must include NYC/LA/Bay Area providers for respective schools |
| **Prescription drug coverage** | Must be included | Reimbursement-only pharmacy plans do not qualify |
| **Preventive care** | Covered at 100% (no cost-sharing) | ACA-compliant plans satisfy this automatically |
| **Coverage period** | Must span the full academic year (Aug/Sep through Aug of next year) | Gaps of even one day can trigger denial |

### 1.2 Additional Requirements for International Students

International students must meet all universal criteria **plus** these additional standards:

| Criteria | Typical Standard | Example Schools |
|---|---|---|
| **U.S.-based insurer** | Policy must be underwritten by a company with a U.S. claims office, address, and phone number | UCLA, UC Berkeley, UCSD, USC, NYU, Stanford, UMich |
| **Policy language** | Complete master policy written in standard English, benefits in U.S. dollars | All UC campuses, USC, Stanford |
| **Medical evacuation** | Minimum $50,000/year | UC Berkeley, USC, Stanford, Columbia |
| **Repatriation of remains** | Minimum $25,000 | UCSD, USC, Stanford, Columbia |
| **Suicide-related services** | Must cover attempted suicide and suicidal ideation treatment | UC Berkeley, UCSD (UC system-wide) |
| **Recreational/sports injury** | Must cover injuries from amateur sports and recreational activities | UC Berkeley, UCSD (UC system-wide) |
| **No reimbursement-only plans** | Plan must pay providers directly; reimbursement programs do not qualify | USC, UC Irvine, UCSD |
| **ACA compliance** | Plan must meet Affordable Care Act minimum essential coverage standards | MIT, USC, many others |
| **Deductible limits** | Varies: $100/year (UMich), $500 (Stanford J-1), $1,000 (Stanford F-1) | See per-school details |

### 1.3 J-1 Visa Holders: Extra Federal Requirements

The U.S. Department of State imposes additional mandates on J-1 exchange visitors:

- Deductible no greater than **$500 per illness/injury**
- Medical benefits of at least **$100,000 per illness/injury**
- Repatriation of remains: minimum **$25,000**
- Medical evacuation: minimum **$50,000**
- Coverage for dependents (J-2 visa holders) is also mandatory

At some schools (Columbia, certain MIT programs), J-1 visa holders **cannot waive** the school plan at all.

## 2. University-Specific Details

### UC System (UCLA, UC Berkeley, UCSD)

The University of California operates a system-wide **UC SHIP** with consistent baseline criteria, though individual campuses may add requirements.

- **Annual premium (2025--26):** ~$6,834 ([UC Berkeley SHIP Fees](https://uhs.berkeley.edu/insurance/ship-fees-enrollment))
- **Waiver period (Berkeley):** May 1 -- July 15 (on time); July 16 -- Aug 15 ($75 late fee); no waivers after Aug 15
- **Network radius:** 50 miles (Berkeley, UCLA) or 40 miles (UCSD)
- **Out-of-pocket max:** $10,600 individual / $21,200 family (Berkeley); $8,700 / $17,400 (UCSD)
- **Verification:** The university contacts your insurer directly to confirm active enrollment
- **Renewal:** Must reapply every academic year

Sources: [UC Berkeley Waiver Requirements](https://uhs.berkeley.edu/insurance-ship/waiving-ship/ship-waiver-requirements) | [UCLA Waiving UCSHIP](https://www.studenthealth.ucla.edu/insurance/benefits/waiving-uc-ship) | [UCSD Waiver Criteria](https://shwadmin.ucsd.edu/uc-ship/waive/waiver-criteria.html)

### USC (University of Southern California)

- **Annual premium:** Comparable to UC system (~$6,000--$7,000 range)
- **Waiver deadlines:** Fall 2025: Sept 12, 2025; Spring 2026: Jan 30, 2026
- **Medical evacuation:** $50,000 minimum
- **Repatriation:** $25,000 minimum
- **Plans explicitly rejected:** International student plans from GBG, PSI, Student Medicover; travel insurance; reimbursement programs; Healthcare Sharing Programs; out-of-state Medicaid (for on-campus residents); regional HMO/EPO without LA network
- **Claims processing:** Must be processed and paid directly to U.S. providers

Sources: [USC Waiver Requirements](https://sites.usc.edu/new-student-health-requirements/costs/insurance-waiver-requirements/) | [USC Student Health](https://studenthealth.usc.edu/waiver-requirements/)

### NYU (New York University)

- **Coverage period:** Fall: Aug 21 -- Aug 20 (next year); Spring: Jan 9 -- Aug 20
- **Insurer requirement:** Must be headquartered and operating in the U.S.
- **Benefit limits:** Maximum benefit payable must be **unlimited**
- **Coverage scope:** Inpatient + outpatient for medical/surgical, mental health, substance abuse in the NYC area, plus lab and radiology
- **Documentation:** Student Acknowledgment and Insurance Carrier Certification Form --- student completes Section A, insurance company completes Section B
- **Submission deadline:** February 10 (spring); removal from Bursar account within 3 business days of approval

Sources: [NYU International Student Waiver Instructions](https://www.nyu.edu/students/health-and-wellness/student-health-insurance/international-student-waiver-application-instructions.html) | [NYU Student Health Insurance](https://www.nyu.edu/students/wellbeing/clinical-services/student-health-insurance.html)

### Columbia University

- **Coverage period:** Aug 15, 2025 -- Aug 14, 2026
- **J-1 restriction:** J-1 visa holders and J-2 dependents **cannot waive** the Columbia plan
- **Documentation:** Must upload a letter from insurer confirming medical evacuation and repatriation coverage
- **Processing time:** 5--7 business days
- **Renewal:** Must resubmit every year

Sources: [Columbia Request a Waiver](https://www.health.columbia.edu/content/request-waiver) | [Columbia Student Insurance Key Information](https://www.health.columbia.edu/content/student-insurance-key-information)

### Stanford University (Cardinal Care)

- **Coverage period:** Sept 1 -- Aug 31
- **Deductible:** Less than $1,000 (F-1); less than $500 (J-1, per State Dept. rules)
- **Benefit minimum:** Lifetime aggregate of $2,000,000 or per-condition minimum of $500,000
- **Scope:** Inpatient + outpatient medical and mental health in the San Francisco Bay Area
- **Documentation:** International Student Insurance Coverage Certification Form, completed with insurer
- **Processing:** Email notification within 10 business days

Sources: [Stanford Waiving Cardinal Care: International Students](https://vaden.stanford.edu/insurance-referral-office/choosing-your-insurance/waiving-cardinal-care-international-students) | [Stanford Cardinal Care Waiver](https://stanford.myahpcare.com/waiver)

### MIT

- **ACA compliance:** Alternative plan must meet Massachusetts minimum standards and ACA Minimum Essential Health Benefits
- **Waiver deadlines:** Aug 1 (fall); Jan 2 (spring) --- late submissions result in partial billing
- **J-1 dependents:** Federal regulations require J-1 holders to purchase hospital insurance for all dependents; MIT strongly recommends purchasing MIT's own plan for families
- **Documentation:** Waiver Request Form submitted to Student Insurance Office

Sources: [MIT Medical Insurance Requirement](https://iso.mit.edu/maintaining-status/medical-insurance-requirement/) | [MIT Understanding Health Insurance](https://health.mit.edu/my-mit/internationals/understanding-insurance)

### University of Michigan

- **Deductible:** No more than $100/year individual ($200/family) --- the strictest threshold of any surveyed school
- **Coverage ratio:** Must cover at least 80% of usual and customary charges in the Ann Arbor area
- **Scope:** Hospital, physician (in/out), ambulance, outpatient labs, X-rays, diagnostics
- **Duration:** Must extend through Aug 31 of the current academic year
- **Explicitly disqualified:** Plans marketed solely to international students that are not underwritten by a U.S.-based company; travel insurance

Sources: [UMich Standards and Waiver](https://internationalcenter.umich.edu/healthins/standards-waiver) | [UMich Alternatives](https://internationalcenter.umich.edu/healthins/alternatives)

## 4. Travel Insurance and Home-Country Plans: Almost Never Qualify

This is the single most important takeaway for international students: **travel insurance and home-country government health plans almost never qualify for a SHIP waiver.**

### Why travel insurance fails

- **Short coverage periods:** Travel policies typically cover 60--90 days, far short of the 12-month academic year requirement.
- **No U.S. claims office:** Most travel insurers are headquartered abroad with no U.S. address.
- **Reimbursement-only model:** Travel insurance reimburses the patient after they pay out of pocket; universities require plans that pay providers directly.
- **No mental health parity:** Travel plans rarely include outpatient mental health, substance abuse, or suicide-related coverage.
- **No ACA compliance:** Travel insurance is not filed with U.S. state insurance regulators and does not meet ACA standards.

USC explicitly states: *"Travel insurance plans and reimbursement programs of any kind do not qualify for waiver."* UC Irvine says: *"Travel plans, reimbursement programs from your home country or home government, and plans owned, headquartered or operated outside of the United States will not qualify."*

### Why home-country plans fail

- Government health plans (China's social insurance, UK NHS, Canada's provincial plans) do not operate in the U.S. and have no U.S. provider network.
- Even if they offer some international coverage, benefits are typically expressed in a foreign currency, policy documents are not in English, and there is no U.S. claims office.
- Coverage is usually limited to emergencies abroad, not comprehensive primary/preventive/mental health care.

### The only exception

NYU notes that for students in the **Freshman First Year Away** program, their insurance company or government-issued health plan must be headquartered in their *home country* rather than the U.S. This is a narrow exception for students studying at NYU's international sites, not at the NYC campus.

Sources: [USC Waiver Requirements](https://sites.usc.edu/new-student-health-requirements/costs/insurance-waiver-requirements/) | [ISOA UCSD Waiver Guide](https://www.isoa.org/blog/ucsd-waiver-requirements-for-international-students) | [UC Irvine Undergraduate Insurance](https://studenthealth.uci.edu/undergraduate-insurance/)

## 5. Common Reasons Waivers Get Denied

Based on data from multiple universities and insurance administrators:

### Plan-Level Issues
1. **Non-U.S. insurer:** The plan is underwritten by a foreign company with no U.S. claims office. This is the #1 reason for international student denials.
2. **Not ACA-compliant:** The plan does not meet Affordable Care Act minimum essential coverage standards.
3. **International student marketing plans:** Plans from providers like GBG, PSI, and Student Medicover that are marketed to international students but are not filed with U.S. regulators.
4. **Healthcare Sharing Programs:** Organizations like Medi-Share or Christian Healthcare Ministries are not insurance and are universally rejected.
5. **Limited network:** Regional HMO/EPO plans with no providers near campus (e.g., a Texas HMO for a student at USC in Los Angeles).
6. **Emergency-only coverage:** Plans that cover only emergency/urgent care, not comprehensive medical and mental health services.

### Coverage Gap Issues
7. **Coverage dates don't match:** Policy starts after the term begins or ends before the academic year closes. Even a one-day gap can trigger denial.
8. **Missing mental health parity:** Plan does not cover outpatient mental health, substance abuse, or suicide-related services at parity with medical benefits.
9. **Dollar maximums:** Plan has per-condition, annual, or lifetime benefit caps.
10. **Missing evacuation/repatriation:** No medical evacuation ($50K) or repatriation of remains ($25K) coverage.

### Documentation Issues
11. **Incomplete forms:** Missing insurance company signature, unsigned certification, or illegible documents.
12. **Wrong form version:** Using a prior year's form or a form from a different campus.
13. **Late submission:** Submitting after the deadline with no late-waiver option available.
14. **Unverifiable coverage:** University contacts insurer and cannot confirm active enrollment.

### Visa-Related Issues
15. **J-1 holders at restrictive schools:** Some schools (Columbia) do not allow J-1 students to waive at all.
16. **J-1 deductible too high:** J-1 plans must have a deductible of $500 or less per federal regulations; many alternative plans exceed this.

Sources: [Church Hill Classics: Common Waiver Denial Reasons](https://www.diplomaframe.com/chc-blog/common-student-waiver-denial-reasons-and-how-to-avoid-them/) | [Gallagher Student: Waiver Denial Explanation](https://www.gallagherstudent.com/miscforms/Waiver%20Denial%20Explanation%20Remediation.pdf) | [ISOA: Waiving Your University Health Plan](https://www.isoa.org/blog/waiving-your-university-health-plan-for-international-students)

## 6. Waiver Deadlines and Documentation

### Typical Timeline

| Phase | Timing | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Enrollment opens** | May--June | Automatic SHIP enrollment; waiver portal opens |
| **On-time deadline** | July 15 (UC) / Aug 1 (MIT) / Sept 12 (USC Fall) | Submit waiver application + documentation |
| **Late deadline** | Aug 15 (UC, +$75 fee) | Some schools allow late submission with penalty |
| **Hard cutoff** | 2--4 weeks after term starts | No waivers accepted after this date |
| **Spring deadlines** | Jan 2 (MIT) / Jan 30 (USC) / Feb 10 (NYU) | Separate deadline for spring-only enrollment |

### Documentation Checklist

Most universities require some combination of:

- [ ] **Insurance card** (front and back)
- [ ] **Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC)** or full policy document
- [ ] **Proof of enrollment** confirming active coverage dates
- [ ] **Insurance Carrier Certification Form** signed by the insurance company (NYU, Stanford)
- [ ] **Letter confirming evacuation/repatriation coverage** (Columbia)
- [ ] **Policy booklet in English** with benefits in USD
- [ ] **U.S. claims office address and phone number**

Waivers must be resubmitted **every academic year** at every surveyed school. Approval in one year does not carry forward.

## 7. Key Takeaways

1. **The bar is high for international students.** Meeting all criteria --- U.S.-based insurer, ACA compliance, mental health parity, evacuation/repatriation, English-language policy, no benefit caps --- eliminates the vast majority of international or travel insurance plans.

2. **Employer-sponsored U.S. plans are the easiest path to waiver.** If a student's spouse or parent works for a U.S. employer and the student is covered as a dependent on a major U.S. carrier (Blue Cross, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare, Kaiser), this typically satisfies all requirements.

3. **Third-party "waiver-compliant" plans exist but require scrutiny.** Companies like ISO, Student Medicover, and others market plans specifically designed to meet university waiver criteria. Students should verify with their specific school before purchasing, as some schools (USC) explicitly reject certain providers.

4. **SHIP is often good value.** At $2,600--$6,800/year with comprehensive coverage, campus clinic access, and no network restrictions near school, SHIP may actually be a better deal than a compliant alternative plan that costs $3,000--$5,000 but offers less coverage.

5. **Start early.** Waiver deadlines can be months before the term starts (May--July for fall). Gathering documentation from a foreign insurer takes time. Missing the deadline means paying for SHIP regardless.

# ACA Marketplace Eligibility for International Students and Non-Resident Aliens

*Research compiled April 2026. Covers F-1, J-1, DACA, and other non-citizen categories.*

---

## Executive Summary

International students on F-1 and J-1 visas occupy a unique position in the U.S. healthcare landscape. During their first five calendar years in the country, they are classified as **non-resident aliens** for tax purposes and are generally **exempt from the ACA individual mandate**. However, they are still considered **"lawfully present"** for immigration purposes, which creates a nuanced -- and often confusing -- set of eligibility rules for marketplace plans, subsidies, and public programs like Medicaid.

---

## 1. Are F-1 and J-1 Students Eligible for ACA Marketplace Plans?

**Yes, but with important caveats.**

F-1 and J-1 visa holders are considered "lawfully present" in the United States. According to [HealthCare.gov](https://www.healthcare.gov/immigrants/immigration-status/), persons with **valid non-immigrant visas, including work or student visas**, qualify as lawfully present and can enroll in Marketplace coverage.

However, there is a critical distinction between **immigration status** (lawfully present) and **tax status** (resident vs. non-resident alien). While F-1 and J-1 holders *can* purchase Marketplace plans, whether they are *required* to and whether they qualify for *subsidies* depends on their tax residency status.

| Visa Type | Lawfully Present? | Can Purchase Marketplace Plan? | Subject to Individual Mandate (First 5 Years)? |
|-----------|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| F-1 (Student) | Yes | Yes | No |
| J-1 (Student) | Yes | Yes | No |
| J-1 (Non-Student, e.g., Au Pair, Intern) | Yes | Yes | No (first 2 years) |
| M-1 (Vocational Student) | Yes | Yes | No |
| Q-1 (Cultural Exchange) | Yes | Yes | No |

**Key takeaway:** F-1 and J-1 students *can* buy Marketplace plans, but during their first five years they are *not required* to, and most choose university-sponsored or private international student insurance instead because they may not qualify for premium subsidies.

Sources: [HealthCare.gov - Immigration Status](https://www.healthcare.gov/immigrants/immigration-status/), [International Student Insurance - ACA Impact](https://www.internationalstudentinsurance.com/explained/the-aca-and-international-students.php), [ISOA - ACA Requirements](https://www.isoa.org/international-students:-affordable-care-act-(aca)-requirements-and-exemptions)

## 2. The Substantial Presence Test

The **Substantial Presence Test (SPT)** is the IRS method for determining whether a foreign national is a **resident alien** or **non-resident alien** for tax purposes. This determination directly affects ACA obligations.

### How the Test Works

You are a **resident alien** for tax purposes if you were physically present in the U.S. for at least:
- **31 days** during the current year, AND
- **183 days** during the 3-year period that includes the current year and the 2 preceding years, counted as:
  - All days present in the current year
  - 1/3 of the days present in the prior year
  - 1/6 of the days present in the year before that

### The "Exempt Individual" Exception for Students

Students on F, J, M, or Q visas are classified as **"exempt individuals"** under IRC Section 7701(b), meaning their days of presence **do not count** toward the Substantial Presence Test for a defined period:

| Visa Category | Exempt Period |
|---|---|
| F-1, M-1, Q-1 (Students) | **5 calendar years** |
| J-1 (Students) | **5 calendar years** |
| J-1 (Non-students: researchers, scholars, trainees) | **2 calendar years** |

**Important:** The 5 years are counted as **calendar years**, not consecutive 365-day periods. Any part of a calendar year counts as one of the five years.

**Example:** An F-1 student who entered the U.S. on August 15, 2020 would be an exempt individual for calendar years 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024. Starting January 1, 2025, their days would begin counting toward the SPT.

### Documentation

Students must file **[Form 8843](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8843)** ("Statement for Exempt Individuals and Individuals with a Medical Condition") with the IRS each year to claim exempt status.

Sources: [IRS - Substantial Presence Test](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/substantial-presence-test), [IRS - Exempt Individual Who Is a Student](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/exempt-individual-who-is-a-student), [UC Berkeley - Substantial Presence Test](https://controller.berkeley.edu/payroll/glacier-tax-compliance-system/substantial-presence-test)

## 3. Transition from Non-Resident to Resident Alien

After the 5-calendar-year exempt period expires, an F-1 student's days of physical presence begin counting toward the SPT. The transition typically works as follows:

### Timeline Example

| Year | Calendar Year | Status | SPT Days Count? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2020 (arrived Aug) | Exempt | No |
| 2 | 2021 | Exempt | No |
| 3 | 2022 | Exempt | No |
| 4 | 2023 | Exempt | No |
| 5 | 2024 | Exempt | No |
| 6 | 2025 | **Starts counting** | Yes |

In year 6, if the student is present for 183+ days (virtually certain for a full-time student), they become a **resident alien for tax purposes** and are subject to the same ACA rules as U.S. citizens.

### Exception Beyond 5 Years

A student *may* continue to be treated as an exempt individual beyond 5 years if they can demonstrate to the IRS that they:
1. **Do not intend to reside permanently** in the U.S., AND
2. Have **substantially complied** with their visa requirements

Factors the IRS considers include whether the student has maintained closer connections to their home country and whether they have taken steps to change from nonimmigrant to permanent resident status (e.g., filing for a green card).

Sources: [IRS - Tax Residency Status Examples](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/tax-residency-status-examples), [IRS Topic 851](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc851)

## 4. Individual Mandate Applicability

### Federal Mandate

The federal individual mandate (Section 5000A of the Internal Revenue Code) originally required most U.S. residents to maintain minimum essential coverage or pay a penalty. Key points for international students:

- **Non-resident aliens are exempt** from the federal individual mandate. Since F-1/J-1 students are non-resident aliens for their first 5 calendar years, they are **not subject to the mandate** during that period.
- The **federal penalty was reduced to $0** starting in 2019 by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act. So even after becoming resident aliens, the practical federal penalty is zero.

### State Mandates

Several states maintain their own individual mandates **with financial penalties**:

| State/Jurisdiction | Mandate Active? | Penalty Model |
|---|:--:|---|
| California | Yes (since 2020) | Follows original ACA formula: $750/adult or 2.5% of income, whichever is greater |
| Massachusetts | Yes | $50/month per individual |
| New Jersey | Yes (since 2019) | Follows ACA formula |
| Rhode Island | Yes | Follows ACA formula |
| Vermont | Yes (reporting only) | No financial penalty |
| Washington, D.C. | Yes | Follows ACA formula |

**For international students:** State mandates generally apply to **residents of the state**, not just tax residents. An F-1 student living in California, New Jersey, or Massachusetts may be subject to the state mandate even if they are a non-resident alien for federal tax purposes. Students should check with their university's international student office or a tax advisor for state-specific guidance.

Sources: [ISOA - ACA Compliant](https://www.isoa.org/blog/what-does-affordable-care-act-aca-compliant-mean), [HealthInsurance.org - Uninsured Penalty](https://www.healthinsurance.org/faqs/is-there-still-a-penalty-for-being-uninsured/), [Venteur - 2026 State Requirements](https://www.venteur.com/blog/states-require-you-to-have-health-insurance)

## 5. Premium Subsidy (Premium Tax Credit) Eligibility

### Requirements

To qualify for the Premium Tax Credit (PTC), an individual must:
1. Be a **U.S. citizen or lawfully present** non-citizen
2. Have income between **100% and 400% of the Federal Poverty Level** (FPL) -- though enhanced subsidies through 2025 extend beyond 400% FPL
3. **File a U.S. tax return** (Form 1040 or 1040-NR)
4. Not be eligible for other qualifying coverage (employer plan, Medicare, Medicaid)

### Implications for International Students

- **Non-resident aliens** who are lawfully present *can* technically qualify for marketplace plans and subsidies. However, there is a **filing complication**: non-resident aliens generally cannot file jointly, and PTC eligibility normally requires a joint return for married individuals. Non-resident aliens who are single can file Form 1040-NR and potentially claim the PTC.
- **Resident aliens** (after 5 years) who meet income requirements are fully eligible for premium subsidies on the same basis as U.S. citizens.
- **Special rule for low-income lawfully present immigrants:** Those who are ineligible for Medicaid solely due to immigration status can qualify for PTC even with income **below 100% FPL** -- a significant benefit not available to U.S. citizens.

### Practical Reality

Most F-1 students in their first 5 years:
- Have **limited income** (often below reporting thresholds)
- Are covered by **university health plans** (which satisfy any coverage requirements)
- Find it more practical to use **international student insurance** than to navigate the Marketplace

Sources: [IRS - PTC Eligibility](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/eligibility-for-the-premium-tax-credit), [KFF - Premium Tax Credits](https://www.kff.org/faqs/faqs-health-insurance-marketplace-and-the-aca/help-paying-marketplace-premiums-the-basics/who-is-eligible-for-marketplace-premium-tax-credits/), [Health Reform Beyond the Basics - PTC](https://www.healthreformbeyondthebasics.org/premium-tax-credits-answers-to-frequently-asked-questions/)

## 6. DACA Student Eligibility

DACA (Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals) recipients have had a turbulent history with ACA eligibility:

### Timeline

| Date | Status |
|---|---|
| 2012-2024 | DACA recipients classified as "lawfully present" but **explicitly excluded** from Marketplace and Medicaid |
| November 2024 | Biden administration rule took effect: DACA recipients became **eligible** for Marketplace plans and premium subsidies |
| August 25, 2025 | Trump administration reversed: DACA recipients **no longer eligible** for Marketplace coverage (with or without subsidies) |

### Current Status (April 2026)

**DACA recipients are NOT eligible** for ACA Marketplace coverage or premium subsidies. They are also ineligible for Medicaid in most states (with limited exceptions for emergency Medicaid and state-funded programs).

### Options for DACA Students

- Purchase **off-marketplace** individual health insurance at full cost
- Enroll in **university-sponsored** student health plans
- Access **community health centers** (Federally Qualified Health Centers serve patients regardless of immigration status)
- Seek **financial assistance** from hospitals (charity care programs)
- In some states, access **state-funded health programs** (e.g., California's Medi-Cal had briefly expanded coverage)

Sources: [HealthInsurance.org - DACA Health Insurance](https://www.healthinsurance.org/blog/daca-health-insurance/), [CMS - DACA Policy](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/biden-harris-administration-finalizes-policies-increase-access-health-coverage-daca-recipients), [Georgetown CHIR - DACA Changes](https://chir.georgetown.edu/in-latest-policy-change-for-daca-recipients-trump-administration-proposes-elimination-of-marketplace-insurance-eligibility/), [NILC - ACA for DACA](https://www.nilc.org/resources/affordable-care-act-for-daca-recipients-a-guide-to-apply/)

## 7. Alternatives If NOT Eligible for ACA Marketplace

For international students who cannot or choose not to use ACA Marketplace plans:

### University-Sponsored Student Health Insurance Plans (SHIPs)

- Most universities **require** international students to have health insurance and offer a SHIP
- These plans are often **automatically billed** unless the student submits a waiver with proof of comparable coverage
- **Pros:** Comprehensive coverage, easy enrollment, campus health center integration
- **Cons:** Expensive (often $2,000-$4,000+ per year)

### Private International Student Insurance

Several providers specialize in plans designed for F-1, J-1, and M-1 visa holders:

| Provider | Starting Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ISO (International Student Insurance) | ~$50/month | Widely accepted for university waivers |
| IMG (Student Journey) | ~$30/month (Lite) | Multiple tiers: Lite, Standard, Platinum |
| Compass Student Insurance | Varies | Designed for F-1, J-1, M-1, H-1B |
| StudentHealthUSA | Varies | Plans specifically for university students |

**Important:** These plans may not be ACA-compliant, but since non-resident alien students are exempt from the ACA mandate during their first 5 years, this is generally acceptable.

### Other Options

- **Home country insurance with international coverage** -- verify it covers U.S. medical costs
- **J-1 sponsor-mandated insurance** -- J-1 exchange programs typically require specific insurance meeting Department of State minimums
- **Community Health Centers (FQHCs)** -- provide care on a sliding-fee scale regardless of insurance or immigration status
- **University health centers** -- many provide basic services included in student fees

Sources: [American Visitor Insurance - Student Insurance](https://www.americanvisitorinsurance.com/blog/health-insurance-for-international-students/compare-best-international-student-health-insurance.asp), [ISOA](https://www.isoa.org/), [StudentHealthUSA](https://www.studenthealthusa.com/)

## 8. Medicaid Eligibility for International Students

### Federal Rules

Under federal law, **most non-citizen immigrants** must have a "qualified immigrant" status AND meet a **5-year waiting period** before they can access full Medicaid benefits. F-1 and J-1 visa holders are generally **not** considered "qualified immigrants" for Medicaid purposes.

**Available to all non-citizens regardless of status:**
- **Emergency Medicaid** -- covers emergency medical conditions
- **Pregnancy-related Medicaid** -- available in many states regardless of immigration status

### State Variations

States have significant latitude in expanding Medicaid access beyond federal minimums:

| State | Program | Eligibility for International Students |
|---|---|---|
| **California** | Medi-Cal | See detailed section below |
| **New York** | Medicaid | Covers lawfully present immigrants without 5-year wait for certain categories |
| **Illinois** | Medicaid | Expanded to cover some immigrants regardless of status |
| **Washington** | Apple Health | Covers some lawfully present immigrants |
| **Oregon** | OHP | Expanded eligibility for certain immigrants |
| **Massachusetts** | MassHealth | Covers lawfully present immigrants including students in some cases |

## 9. California Medi-Cal: A Detailed Case Study

California has been the most aggressive state in expanding health coverage to immigrants, making it especially relevant for international students in the state.

### Expansion Timeline

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| Pre-2020 | Undocumented children under 19 eligible |
| May 2022 | Adults 50+ eligible regardless of immigration status |
| January 2024 | **Full expansion**: Adults ages 26-49 eligible regardless of immigration status |
| January 2026 | **Freeze**: New undocumented adult applicants (19+) can only get Emergency or pregnancy-related Medi-Cal |

### Current Status (April 2026)

- **Existing enrollees** who signed up before January 1, 2026 **retain full-scope Medi-Cal** as long as they continue to meet other eligibility requirements (income, residency)
- **New applicants** who are undocumented or lack a qualifying immigration status can only access **Emergency Medi-Cal** or **pregnancy-related Medi-Cal**
- **Children under 19** remain eligible for full-scope Medi-Cal regardless of immigration status
- **Pregnant individuals** remain eligible regardless of immigration status

### F-1 Students and Medi-Cal

F-1 students have a complicated relationship with Medi-Cal:

- They are "lawfully present" but may not qualify for full-scope federal Medicaid due to their non-immigrant status
- During the 2024-2025 expansion period, some F-1 students in California who met income requirements could access full-scope Medi-Cal
- As of 2026, new F-1 applicants may face restrictions depending on how the state categorizes their status
- **Public charge concern:** While Medi-Cal use (except long-term institutional care) is generally **not** counted in public charge determinations, F-1 students should consult an immigration attorney before enrolling, as policies can change

Sources: [DHCS - Medi-Cal Immigrant Eligibility FAQs](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/keep-your-Medi-Cal/Pages/Medi-Cal-Immigrant-Eligibility-FAQs.aspx), [DHCS - Immigration Status Categories](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/Medi-Cal/Pages/immigration-status-categories.aspx), [DHCS - Adult Expansion](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/medi-cal/eligibility/Pages/Adult-Expansion.aspx), [PPIC - Medi-Cal Expansion](https://www.ppic.org/blog/californias-medi-cal-expansion-is-lowering-poverty-among-undocumented-immigrants/)

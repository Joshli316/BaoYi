# Medical Scenario Cost Estimates for International Students in the US

**Purpose:** Help international students understand what common medical situations actually cost in the US healthcare system, with and without insurance coverage.

**Data basis:** Approximate 2024-2025 pricing drawn from published hospital chargemasters, CMS data, fair health databases, and university SHIP plan summaries. All figures are estimates -- actual costs vary significantly by region, provider, and insurance plan.

---

## How US Medical Billing Works (60-Second Primer)

Before the cost tables, you need to understand four terms:

- **Billed/Charged Amount** -- The hospital's sticker price. Almost nobody pays this. It is the starting point for negotiation.
- **Deductible** -- The amount you pay out of pocket before insurance starts covering anything. University SHIP plans typically have $100-$500 annual deductibles.
- **Copay** -- A flat fee you pay per visit (e.g., $25 for primary care, $50 for urgent care, $150-$250 for ER).
- **Coinsurance** -- After you meet your deductible, you pay a percentage (usually 10-20%) and insurance pays the rest.

University SHIP (Student Health Insurance Plans) typically have **in-network** and **out-of-network** rates. Always go in-network. Out-of-network costs can be 2-5x higher.

## Scenario 1: Flu/Cold Visit

**What happens:** You see a primary care doctor or campus health center. They do a rapid flu test, diagnose you, and write a prescription (Tamiflu, antibiotics, or cough medicine).

| Cost Component | Billed Amount | With SHIP | With Travel Insurance | Without Insurance | What Student Pays (SHIP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Office visit (Level 3) | $150-$300 | Covered after copay | May cover 80% after deductible | $150-$300 | $20-$35 copay |
| Rapid flu/strep test | $25-$75 | Covered | Usually covered | $25-$75 | $0 (included in copay visit) |
| Prescription (Tamiflu) | $100-$175 | $10-$30 generic copay | Rarely covered | $100-$175 | $10-$30 |
| **Total** | **$275-$550** | **$30-$65** | **$50-$150** | **$275-$550** | **$30-$65** |

**Pro tip:** Many campus health centers offer free or very low-cost visits ($0-$15) for enrolled students, independent of your insurance. Go there first.

## Scenario 2: Urgent Care Visit

**What happens:** You sprain your ankle playing basketball, get a minor cut that needs stitches, or have a UTI. You go to an urgent care clinic (not the ER).

| Cost Component | Billed Amount | With SHIP | With Travel Insurance | Without Insurance | What Student Pays (SHIP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urgent care visit | $200-$400 | Covered after copay | 80% after deductible | $200-$400 | $35-$75 copay |
| X-ray (ankle, 2 views) | $150-$400 | Covered (10-20% coinsurance) | Usually covered | $150-$400 | $0-$40 |
| Splint/wrap/stitches | $100-$300 | Covered | Usually covered | $100-$300 | $0-$30 |
| Prescription (antibiotics for UTI) | $10-$50 | $10-$20 generic copay | Rarely covered | $10-$50 | $10-$20 |
| **Total** | **$460-$1,150** | **$45-$165** | **$100-$300** | **$460-$1,150** | **$45-$165** |

**Why this matters:** Urgent care is 3-10x cheaper than the ER for the same injury. If it is not life-threatening, go to urgent care.

## Scenario 3: ER Visit -- Chest Pain Workup

**What happens:** You have chest pain. You go to the ER. They do an EKG, blood work (troponin, CBC, metabolic panel), chest X-ray, and observe you for 3-6 hours. Diagnosis: anxiety/acid reflux (most common outcome for young adults).

| Cost Component | Billed Amount | With SHIP | With Travel Insurance | Without Insurance | What Student Pays (SHIP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ER facility fee | $1,500-$3,500 | Covered after copay | 80% after deductible | $1,500-$3,500 | $150-$300 copay |
| Physician fee | $500-$1,200 | Covered (10-20% coinsurance) | Usually covered | $500-$1,200 | $50-$120 |
| EKG | $150-$500 | Covered | Usually covered | $150-$500 | $15-$50 |
| Blood work (panel) | $200-$800 | Covered | Usually covered | $200-$800 | $20-$80 |
| Chest X-ray | $200-$500 | Covered | Usually covered | $200-$500 | $20-$50 |
| Observation (3-6 hrs) | $500-$2,000 | Covered | May be covered | $500-$2,000 | $50-$200 |
| **Total** | **$3,050-$8,500** | **$305-$800** | **$600-$2,000** | **$3,050-$8,500** | **$305-$800** |

**Critical note:** If you are admitted to observation status (not formally "admitted" to the hospital), insurance treats it differently. Some plans cover observation at the ER copay rate; others apply inpatient coinsurance. Ask before you leave.

## Scenario 5: Appendectomy

**What happens:** Sudden abdominal pain. ER visit, CT scan confirms appendicitis. Emergency laparoscopic surgery, 1-2 day hospital stay.

| Cost Component | Billed Amount | With SHIP | With Travel Insurance | Without Insurance | What Student Pays (SHIP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ER visit + workup | $3,000-$6,000 | Covered after copay | 80% after deductible | $3,000-$6,000 | $150-$300 |
| CT scan (abdomen) | $1,500-$4,000 | Covered (10-20% coinsurance) | Usually covered | $1,500-$4,000 | $150-$400 |
| Surgeon fee | $3,000-$8,000 | Covered | Usually covered | $3,000-$8,000 | $300-$800 |
| Anesthesia | $1,500-$3,000 | Covered | Usually covered | $1,500-$3,000 | $150-$300 |
| Hospital stay (1-2 days) | $5,000-$15,000 | Covered | Usually covered | $5,000-$15,000 | $500-$1,500 |
| Pathology | $300-$800 | Covered | Usually covered | $300-$800 | $30-$80 |
| Medications (IV antibiotics, pain) | $500-$2,000 | Covered | Usually covered | $500-$2,000 | $50-$200 |
| **Total** | **$14,800-$38,800** | **$1,330-$3,580** | **$3,000-$8,000** | **$14,800-$38,800** | **$1,330-$3,580** |

**The safety net:** Most SHIP plans have an annual out-of-pocket maximum of $4,000-$8,000. For a major event like this, you will likely hit that cap, meaning insurance covers 100% of remaining costs for the rest of the plan year.

**Travel insurance warning:** Many travel insurance plans have coverage caps of $50,000-$100,000. An appendectomy with complications could approach or exceed that. Travel insurance is NOT a substitute for proper health insurance.

## Scenario 6: Therapy Session

**What happens:** You want to see a therapist for anxiety, depression, adjustment issues, or homesickness. Individual 50-minute session with a licensed therapist (LCSW, LMFT, or psychologist).

| Cost Component | Billed Amount | With SHIP | With Travel Insurance | Without Insurance | What Student Pays (SHIP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual therapy (50 min, LCSW/LMFT) | $150-$250 | Covered after copay | Almost never covered | $150-$250 | $20-$40 copay |
| Individual therapy (50 min, PhD psychologist) | $200-$350 | Covered after copay | Almost never covered | $200-$350 | $25-$50 copay |
| Psychiatrist (medication management, 20 min) | $250-$450 | Covered after specialist copay | Almost never covered | $250-$450 | $35-$75 copay |
| Campus counseling center | Free-$25 | Free (included in student fees) | N/A | Free-$25 | $0 |

**Session limits:** Some SHIP plans limit therapy to 20-30 sessions per year. Some have no limit. Check your plan. Campus counseling centers typically offer 6-12 free sessions and then refer you to an outside provider.

**What to do first:** Start at your campus counseling center. It is free, confidential, and they can help you navigate getting a longer-term therapist covered by your SHIP plan.

## Scenario 7: Dental Emergency

**What happens:** Severe toothache. You need an emergency dental visit and a tooth extraction. Most health insurance (including SHIP) does NOT cover dental care.

| Cost Component | Billed Amount | With SHIP | With Dental Plan | Without Insurance | What Student Pays |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency dental exam | $75-$200 | **Not covered** | Covered after copay | $75-$200 | $75-$200 |
| Dental X-ray (periapical) | $25-$75 | **Not covered** | Usually covered | $25-$75 | $25-$75 |
| Simple extraction | $150-$350 | **Not covered** | 50-80% covered | $150-$350 | $150-$350 |
| Surgical extraction (impacted) | $300-$650 | **Not covered** | 50% covered | $300-$650 | $300-$650 |
| Prescription (antibiotics + pain) | $20-$60 | $10-$20 (medication IS covered by health insurance) | N/A | $20-$60 | $10-$20 |
| **Total (simple extraction)** | **$270-$685** | **$10-$20 (meds only)** | **$75-$200** | **$270-$685** | **$260-$645** |

**Key point:** Dental insurance is separate from health insurance. Some universities offer optional dental plans ($200-$500/year). If you have known dental issues, buy it. If your university does not offer one, look into dental schools -- they offer services at 50-70% off regular prices, performed by supervised students.

**Exception:** If a dental emergency involves your jaw (fracture) or is caused by an accident, your health insurance/SHIP may cover it as a medical claim.

## Scenario 8: Annual Physical / Preventive Care

**What happens:** Routine annual exam. Doctor checks vitals, does a basic physical, orders standard blood work (CBC, metabolic panel, lipid panel). Possibly a TB test (required by some universities).

| Cost Component | Billed Amount | With SHIP | With Travel Insurance | Without Insurance | What Student Pays (SHIP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Annual physical exam | $200-$400 | **$0** (preventive = no copay under ACA) | Usually not covered | $200-$400 | **$0** |
| Blood work (CBC, CMP, lipid panel) | $100-$400 | **$0** (preventive) | Usually not covered | $100-$400 | **$0** |
| TB test (PPD or blood) | $25-$100 | **$0** (preventive) | Usually not covered | $25-$100 | **$0** |
| Immunizations (flu shot, etc.) | $25-$75 each | **$0** (preventive) | Usually not covered | $25-$75 | **$0** |
| **Total** | **$350-$975** | **$0** | **Not typically covered** | **$350-$975** | **$0** |

**This is the good news:** Under the Affordable Care Act (ACA), all compliant health plans (including university SHIP plans) must cover preventive care at $0 cost-sharing. This includes annual physicals, immunizations, screenings, and basic blood work. Take advantage of this every year.

**Warning:** If the doctor finds something during your physical and orders additional diagnostic tests, those tests may NOT be covered as preventive. Ask your doctor to code it correctly.

## Scenario 9: Prescription Medications

**What happens:** You need ongoing or one-time prescriptions. Cost depends heavily on whether the drug is generic, preferred brand, or specialty.

| Medication Type | Retail Price (no insurance) | SHIP Copay (generic) | SHIP Copay (brand) | Travel Insurance | Student Pays (SHIP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antibiotics (amoxicillin, 10-day) | $10-$30 | $5-$15 | N/A (generic available) | Rarely covered | $5-$15 |
| Birth control (generic pill, 1 month) | $20-$50 | **$0** (ACA mandate) | **$0** | Not covered | **$0** |
| Allergy meds (cetirizine/loratadine, OTC) | $10-$25 | Not covered (OTC) | N/A | Not covered | $10-$25 (buy OTC) |

| Inhaler (albuterol, rescue) | $25-$80 | $10-$25 | N/A | Rarely covered | $10-$25 |

**Money-saving tips:**
- Always ask for generic. It is chemically identical and 50-90% cheaper.
- Use GoodRx (goodrx.com) to compare pharmacy prices -- sometimes the GoodRx coupon price is cheaper than your insurance copay.
- Costco pharmacy does not require a membership and often has the lowest prices.
- Some universities have on-campus pharmacies with discounted pricing for students.
- Birth control pills are free under all ACA-compliant plans. No copay, no deductible.

## Regional Cost Variation

Costs vary dramatically by location:

- **High-cost areas** (NYC, SF Bay Area, Boston, LA): Add 30-60% to the mid-range estimates above.
- **Mid-cost areas** (Chicago, Seattle, DC, Denver): Use the mid-range estimates.
- **Lower-cost areas** (Midwest, South, rural areas): Subtract 20-40% from the mid-range estimates.

Hospital type also matters:
- **Academic medical centers** (university hospitals): Higher billed prices, but often better insurance negotiated rates and more financial assistance programs.
- **Community hospitals:** Moderate pricing.
- **For-profit hospitals:** Often the highest prices with the least financial assistance.

## Five Things Every International Student Must Do

1. **Understand your SHIP plan before you need it.** Read the Summary of Benefits. Know your deductible, copays, coinsurance, and out-of-pocket maximum. Know how to find in-network providers.

2. **Use campus health services first.** They are cheaper, faster, and designed for students. Many visits are free or very low cost, separate from your insurance.

3. **Never go to the ER for non-emergencies.** Urgent care costs 3-10x less than the ER for the same problem. Only go to the ER for: chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, head injuries, signs of stroke, severe allergic reactions, or broken bones with visible deformity.

4. **Always ask: "Is this provider in-network?"** One question that can save you thousands of dollars. Call the number on the back of your insurance card or check the online provider directory.

5. **If you get a large bill, do not panic.** You can negotiate. Ask for an itemized bill, request a payment plan, apply for financial assistance (most hospitals have charity care programs), and dispute any errors. Bills are negotiable -- this is normal in the US system.

# J-1 Visa Health Insurance Requirements Under 22 CFR 62.14

## Overview

The U.S. Department of State mandates that all J-1 exchange visitors and their J-2 dependents maintain health insurance meeting specific minimum standards for the entire duration of their exchange program. These requirements are codified in [22 CFR Section 62.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.14) and are enforced through the Exchange Visitor Program (EVP). Unlike F-1 student visas, J-1 insurance requirements carry the force of federal law -- non-compliance can result in immediate program termination.

The current minimum coverage thresholds took effect on **May 15, 2015**, when the State Department significantly increased the prior minimums (which had been $50,000 medical / $7,500 repatriation / $10,000 evacuation).

---

## Exact Minimum Coverage Requirements

### Coverage Thresholds

| Requirement | Minimum Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Medical benefits | **$100,000** per accident or illness | Per-occurrence, not annual aggregate |
| Repatriation of remains | **$25,000** | Covers return of remains to home country in case of death |
| Medical evacuation | **$50,000** | Emergency transport to home country |
| Maximum deductible | **$500** per accident or illness | Per-occurrence deductible cap |
| Maximum co-insurance | **25%** | Policy may require up to 25% co-pay |
| Pre-existing conditions | Reasonable waiting period allowed | Must cover after waiting period |
| Activity exclusions | Cannot unreasonably exclude | Must cover perils inherent to program activities |

Source: [22 CFR 62.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.14); [Duke Visa Services](https://visaservices.duke.edu/visa-categories/j-1/required-insurance-specifications-under-j-1-visa-regulations/); [UVA ISSP](https://issp.virginia.edu/health-insurance-requirement-j-1-and-j-2)

### Insurance Company Rating Requirements

The underwriting insurer must hold at least one of the following financial strength ratings:

| Rating Agency | Minimum Rating |
|---|---|
| A.M. Best | A- or above |
| Standard & Poor's (McGraw Hill Financial) | A- or above |
| Fitch Ratings | A- or above |
| Moody's Investor Services | A3 or above |
| Weiss Research, Inc. | B+ or above |

Alternatively, the policy may be backed by the **full faith and credit of the exchange visitor's home country government**.

Source: [22 CFR 62.14(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.14); [MIT ISO](https://ischo.mit.edu/pre-arrival-information/health-insurance-requirements-j-1-scholars-and-j-2-dependents)

## What "Per Accident or Illness" Means in Practice

The $100,000 coverage minimum and $500 deductible cap are both measured **per accident or illness** -- meaning per occurrence, not per year or per lifetime. This is a critical distinction:

- **Each separate medical event** (a broken arm, a case of pneumonia, appendicitis) is treated as its own occurrence with up to $100,000 in coverage.
- **Each occurrence carries its own deductible** of up to $500. If you have three separate illnesses in one year, you could pay up to $1,500 in deductibles total ($500 x 3).
- This structure is **more protective than an annual aggregate** for catastrophic events, because a single $100,000 hospitalization does not consume a shared pool that other conditions draw from.
- However, it also means **each new condition resets the deductible clock** -- you pay the out-of-pocket amount for every distinct incident.

Many compliant J-1 plans actually exceed this minimum, offering $250,000 or even $500,000 per occurrence, or unlimited lifetime maximums.

## Coverage Period

Insurance must be in effect for the **entire program duration** as recorded in SEVIS (the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System). Specifically:

- Coverage must begin on the **program start date** listed on the DS-2019 form.
- Coverage must continue through the **program end date**, regardless of geographic location during that period.
- If a J-1 visitor arrives in the U.S. before the official program start date, or departs after the end date, they should secure **separate coverage for those gap periods**.
- Sponsors may offer optional "entry to exit" coverage that extends beyond the formal program dates.

Source: [Berkeley International Office](https://internationaloffice.berkeley.edu/students/current/j-1/insurance); [UVA ISSP](https://issp.virginia.edu/health-insurance-requirement-j-1-and-j-2)

## J-2 Dependent Requirements

J-2 dependents (spouses and children) must maintain **identical coverage** to the J-1 principal visitor. There is no reduced standard or exemption for dependents.

| Aspect | J-1 Principal | J-2 Dependent |
|---|---|---|
| Medical benefits minimum | $100,000 per accident/illness | $100,000 per accident/illness |
| Repatriation of remains | $25,000 | $25,000 |
| Medical evacuation | $50,000 | $50,000 |
| Maximum deductible | $500 per accident/illness | $500 per accident/illness |
| Coverage period | Full program duration | Full program duration |
| Consequence of lapse | Program termination | Program termination (of J-1 holder) |

Key points:

- The J-1 sponsor must provide **written advance notification** to exchange visitors about dependent insurance requirements (22 CFR 62.14(h)).
- If **any** J-2 dependent lacks compliant coverage, the J-1 principal's program is subject to termination -- not just the dependent's status.
- "Every J-2 dependent with you in the U.S." must obtain identical coverage, per university compliance offices.

Source: [UVA ISSP](https://issp.virginia.edu/health-insurance-requirement-j-1-and-j-2); [UT Dallas ISSO](https://isso.utdallas.edu/immigration-status/maintain-j-1/j-1-insurance/); [Emory ISSS](https://isss.emory.edu/life-in-the-us/j-1j-2-health-insurance-requirements)

## The "Willful Failure" Clause

Section 62.14(i) and (j) contain the enforcement teeth of the regulation:

> **"Willful failure to carry insurance is considered a violation of your legal status and can result in termination of your program."**

### What constitutes "willful failure"

- Knowingly allowing insurance to lapse without obtaining replacement coverage.
- Deliberately purchasing a non-compliant plan to save money.
- Ignoring sponsor notifications about coverage deficiencies.
- Making **material misrepresentations** about insurance compliance (e.g., submitting false proof of coverage).

### Mandatory sponsor action

Under 22 CFR 62.14(j), sponsors **must** terminate an exchange visitor's participation if the sponsor determines that the visitor or any accompanying spouse or dependent willfully fails to remain in compliance. This is not discretionary -- the regulation uses the word "must," making termination mandatory upon a finding of willful non-compliance.

### Consequences of termination

- Immediate loss of J-1 status and DS-2019 validity.
- Loss of employment authorization for both J-1 and J-2 dependents.
- Reporting to the Department of State through SEVIS.
- Jeopardized affiliation with the host institution.
- Potential difficulty obtaining future U.S. visas due to the termination record.

Source: [22 CFR 62.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.14); [NYU ISS](https://www.nyu.edu/faculty/visa-and-immigration/international-scholar-services/visa-types/j-1/J1healthinsurancerequirementinfo.html); [UC Denver Insurance Policy](https://www.ucdenver.edu/docs/librariesprovider190/default-document-library/j1_insurance_policy.pdf)

## How J-1 Sponsors Verify Compliance

Sponsors are responsible for ensuring compliance, and verification methods vary by institution:

### Common verification approaches

1. **Check-in documentation**: Most universities require exchange visitors to upload proof of insurance during orientation or check-in, with English-language confirmation that the policy meets all stated requirements.

2. **Annual or semester re-verification**: Many sponsors require updated proof of coverage at the start of each academic term or annually.

3. **Approved provider lists**: Some sponsors maintain lists of pre-approved insurance plans known to meet 22 CFR 62.14 requirements, simplifying verification.

4. **Automatic enrollment**: Universities like UC Berkeley automatically enroll J-1 students in the Student Health Insurance Program (SHIP), which is designed to meet federal requirements. Students may waive SHIP only if they prove alternative compliant coverage.

5. **Registration holds**: Non-compliant students may face registration holds preventing course enrollment until insurance documentation is provided.

### What sponsors must verify

- Coverage meets all four minimum thresholds ($100K / $25K / $50K / $500 deductible).
- The underwriting insurer holds an acceptable financial rating.
- Coverage is continuous for the full program duration.
- J-2 dependents, if any, also have compliant coverage.

Sponsors **cannot charge fees** beyond demonstrable staff time for insurance verification services (22 CFR 62.14(b)). They must also provide exchange visitors with the option to arrange their own coverage rather than being forced into a sponsor-selected plan, though payroll deduction for insurance requires written authorization.

## Consequences for Sponsors

Sponsors who fail to enforce insurance compliance face serious consequences under [22 CFR Part 62, Subpart D (Sanctions)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-62/subpart-D) and [Subpart E (Termination and Revocation)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-62/subpart-E):

| Sanction Level | Description |
|---|---|
| Written reprimand | Warning of potential suspension or revocation |
| Probation | Program placed on probation for a period determined by the Department |
| Suspension | Temporary inability to issue DS-2019 forms or accept new participants |
| Revocation | Permanent loss of Exchange Visitor Program designation |

### Revocation process

- The Department provides **at least 30 days' written notice** of intent to revoke.
- The sponsor has **10 days** to submit a statement in opposition or mitigation.
- Revocation **need not be preceded** by any lesser sanction -- it can be the first action taken.
- A revoked sponsor **cannot apply for redesignation for 5 years**.
- Revoked sponsors cannot issue DS-2019 forms, advertise, recruit for, or promote their program.
- Revocation does **not diminish** the sponsor's legal or financial responsibilities to existing participants.

Source: [22 CFR 62.50](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.50); [22 CFR 62.63](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-62/subpart-E/section-62.63)

## J-1 vs. F-1 Insurance Requirements

This is one of the most important distinctions in international student insurance compliance:

| Dimension | J-1 Exchange Visitor | F-1 Student |
|---|---|---|
| **Federal mandate** | Yes -- 22 CFR 62.14 | **No federal requirement** |
| **Regulatory authority** | U.S. Department of State | None (insurance is institutional policy) |
| **Minimum medical coverage** | $100,000 per accident/illness | Set by individual schools (varies widely) |
| **Repatriation of remains** | $25,000 required | Rarely required by schools |
| **Medical evacuation** | $50,000 required | Rarely required by schools |
| **Maximum deductible** | $500 per accident/illness | No federal cap |
| **Insurer rating requirement** | A.M. Best A- or equivalent | No federal requirement |
| **Non-compliance consequence** | Mandatory program termination | Registration hold, school-level discipline |
| **Government reporting** | Termination reported via SEVIS | No government reporting for insurance |
| **Dependent coverage** | Required by law for J-2 | No federal requirement for F-2 |
| **Flexibility** | Must meet rigid federal minimums | Students can shop for cheaper plans meeting school rules |

### Why this matters

- **F-1 students** face no federal insurance mandate whatsoever. Their requirements come entirely from their school's institutional policies, which vary significantly. Some schools require comprehensive coverage; others have minimal requirements. F-1 students generally have more flexibility to shop for affordable plans.

- **J-1 exchange visitors** operate under a federal floor that cannot be waived by any sponsor. Even if a university has lower institutional insurance standards, J-1 visitors must meet the higher federal minimums. The consequence of non-compliance is a federal action (program termination reported to State Department), not merely a school administrative action.

- Many universities set their institutional insurance requirements to match or exceed 22 CFR 62.14 standards for all international students, effectively eliminating the practical difference. But the legal distinction remains significant: a J-1 visitor's insurance lapse is a federal compliance violation, while an F-1 student's lapse is an institutional policy violation.

Source: [AcademiQuirk J-1/F-1 Comparison](https://academiquirk.com/article/us-international-student-insurance-j1-f1-compared-australian-students-2026/); [AmeriGo Education F-1 Guide](https://www.amerigoeducation.com/news/health-insurance-for-f1-students)

## Additional Regulatory Details

### Self-Insurance Options

- **Government entities**: Federal, state, and local government agencies and public colleges/universities may self-insure J-1 visitors if permitted by law (22 CFR 62.14(e)).
- **Non-governmental sponsors**: The Department of State may permit self-insurance upon demonstration of available funds (22 CFR 62.14(f)).
- **Payment bonds**: The Department may require payment bonds guaranteeing sponsor insurance obligations (22 CFR 62.14(g)).

### ACA (Affordable Care Act) Interaction

Sponsors must inform exchange visitors about potential Affordable Care Act requirements that may apply to them during their stay (22 CFR 62.14(a)). The ACA's individual mandate (now effectively $0 penalty at the federal level) and marketplace options may be relevant depending on program duration and state of residence.

## Key Takeaways

1. **J-1 insurance requirements are federal law**, not institutional policy. The four minimums ($100K medical / $25K repatriation / $50K evacuation / $500 deductible) are non-negotiable.
2. **J-2 dependents must carry identical coverage** -- a lapse by any dependent triggers the J-1 holder's program termination.
3. **"Per accident or illness"** means per occurrence. Each separate medical event has its own $100K coverage limit and up to $500 deductible.
4. **Willful failure is grounds for mandatory termination** -- sponsors have no discretion; they must terminate and report.
5. **F-1 students have no equivalent federal mandate** -- this is a J-1-specific regulatory framework.
6. **Sponsors face severe consequences** for non-enforcement, up to 5-year revocation of their program designation.
7. **Always verify compliance** with your specific sponsor. Use their approved provider list or submit documentation for review before assuming a plan qualifies.
